I was knee deep in IR blasters for a while.

It started simple — I wanted to make my TV smart. Send power on, change inputs, adjust volume, all from Home Assistant. I built a transmitter with an ESP8266 and an IR LED, pointed it at the TV, and cloned the remote codes.

But then I realized I could also control the AC in the bedroom. Same principle — capture the remote codes, replay them from an ESP. The AC remotes are more complex than TV remotes (they send the entire state — temp, mode, fan speed — in every burst), but once you decode the protocol it's straightforward.

Then it got more interesting. I started playing with IR *receive* in a separate area, so I could use the TV remote to trigger Home Assistant automations. Point the remote at a sensor, press a button, and it fires a webhook. Turn on the AC from the couch. Toggle lights. Whatever you want.

I had so many different ones at one point. Different form factors, different ESP boards, different IR LED arrangements. Some with a single LED for targeted control, some with multiple LEDs for wide-angle coverage. I was building them faster than I had things to point them at.
