I was so sick of carrying dongles. Two laptops, neither with USB-C, and a bag full of adapters. So I decided to just add the ports myself.

## The Acer

The Acer was the easier one. There's so much empty space inside these things once you take the bottom panel off. I found a spot near the existing port cluster, drilled out a clean opening, and mounted a USB-C breakout board. Soldered the data lines to an internal USB header and wired up the power delivery circuit.

## The Lenovo

The Lenovo was tight. Really tight. I had to go back twice to fix it for good. The first attempt worked but the fit wasn't reliable — the port would lose connection if you bumped the cable. Second time I reinforced the mounting and redid the solder joints.

## What I Learned About USB-C PD

This is where it got genuinely interesting. USB-C Power Delivery doesn't just send power — the device and charger negotiate voltage and current through a handshake protocol. At the simplest level, a specific resistance value on the CC (Configuration Channel) pins tells the charger what voltage to provide. Different resistor values request different voltage levels — 5V, 9V, 15V, 20V.

My first approach was taking apart one of my existing dongles to harvest the controller board. That worked, but it was messy and the boards were tiny. I found it much easier to buy fresh USB-C breakout boards and solder my own resistors inline to set the PD voltage to match each laptop's charging spec.

The resistor is the handshake. No chip needed for basic PD negotiation — just the right resistance on CC1, and the charger delivers the voltage you asked for.
