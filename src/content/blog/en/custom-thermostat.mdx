This was a full end-to-end project. No kits, no shortcuts — just components, a breadboard, and a lot of free time.

## The Wiring

I started with test wiring on breadboards using dupont cables. I'd never had to figure out wiring from scratch like this before — figuring out which pins go where, what needs pull-up resistors, what voltage each component expects. I learned as I went.

Once the circuits were working on the breadboard, I moved them to perfboard and soldered everything in place. That transition from "it works with jumper wires" to "it works permanently" is its own skill. Solder bridges, cold joints, traces that look good but aren't connected — I hit all of them.

## The OLED Screen

Programming the OLED display was done with a single library and a lot of patience. Drawing text, formatting temperatures, updating the display without flickering. Not hard, just tedious.

## Rotary Encoder Hell

Getting the rotary encoder to work was a lesson in why people don't do this from scratch.

First problem: bouncing. A single click of the encoder generates multiple electrical signals because the mechanical contacts literally bounce. You have to debounce it — filter out the noise and only register intentional turns. I tried doing it myself. Gave up and grabbed a library.

Second problem: the main loop. Sometimes the application was busy doing something else — reading a sensor, updating the display, handling MQTT — and it would miss the rotary encoder input entirely. I had to set up two hardware interrupts to always prioritize the encoder. Even then, it wasn't perfect. You'd turn the knob and nothing would happen, or it would register two clicks instead of one.

## The Relay

The thermostat itself was simple once all the input/output stuff worked. It's just a fancy relay — read the temperature, compare it to the setpoint, turn the heater on or off. The hard part was everything around it.

## MQTT and Home Assistant

Then I added MQTT — from scratch, no Tasmota, no ESPHome. Wired it into Home Assistant manually. It was a pain. Got it working with a JavaScript library that rendered a Nest-style thermostat dial in the browser, and did the plumbing with a combination of Jinja templates, JavaScript, and Node-RED.

Node-RED read from the MQTT topics and handled all the thermostat logic — comparing setpoint to actual temp, deciding when to fire the relay. The JavaScript Nest-style dial rendered in a separate browser window, which I then embedded into Home Assistant as an iframe. It worked. But it was fragile, hard to maintain, and Home Assistant's built-in thermostat entity was just better in every way.

## The Tasmota Rewrite

Later I redid the whole thing with Tasmota and some backend automations in Home Assistant. What took me weeks the first time took about a day. Tasmota handled the MQTT, the sensor reading, and the relay control. Home Assistant handled the UI and the logic. Done.

## The Punchline

The dream was to get the rotary encoder working both locally and synced with Home Assistant — turn the knob on the wall and see the change on your phone, change the temp on your phone and see the display update on the wall. I got it mostly working, but honestly, using my phone was better in every way. Faster, more reliable, accessible from anywhere in the house.

I built a thermostat to avoid using my phone. Then I used my phone anyway — the phone talks to Home Assistant, Home Assistant talks to the thermostat over WiFi via MQTT. The wall unit still works, but why walk to it when the same control is in your pocket?
