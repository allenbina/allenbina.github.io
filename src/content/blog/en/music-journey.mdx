No one in my family is into music. Not really. So when I picked up the tuba in high school, it was entirely my own thing. I played through high school and into college at UCLA with the Bruin marching band. It wasn't cool, it wasn't glamorous, but it was the entry point. That's where I discovered I genuinely loved music — not just listening to it, but understanding how it worked.

After college the tuba stayed in the case, but the love for music didn't go anywhere. I got into vinyl DJing. Started collecting records, learning to beatmatch, buying gear. It became a serious hobby. The collection grew to about 3,000 records — enough to fill a living room wall.

![The DJ setup — turntables and mixer](/images/blog/music-journey/IMG_20200222_180304_dj_full.jpg)

![Turntable close-up with custom modifications](/images/blog/music-journey/IMG_20200222_180544_dj_turntable.jpg)

Eventually I went digital too. Picked up a controller just to play with at home. The vinyl setup is still there but the controller is more practical for making playlists and mixing casually.

![Record collection — a wall of vinyl](/images/blog/music-journey/IMG_20200222_180417_records.jpg)

I haven't really DJ'd out in years. But I still do it at home by myself — headphones on, making mixes, digging through new music. It's the same thing it always was. Just me and the music.
