This is the story of every cooling mistake I've made, in order.

## Case Fans and the Window

It started with adding fans to the top of my case. More airflow, lower temps, simple. Then I cut a window into the side panel to show off my Zalman flower cooler — the copper one that looked like a sunflower. It was beautiful. It deserved to be seen.

## Peltier Cooling

Then I discovered peltier coolers. A thermoelectric plate that gets ice cold on one side and scorching hot on the other. Strap it between the CPU and a heatsink and you get sub-ambient temperatures. On paper.

In practice, I was living in fear. Peltier coolers condensate. Cold surface plus warm humid air equals water droplets on your motherboard. I was slathering everything in silicone grease as insulation, hoping I didn't wake up to a dead system. It worked, temps were great, but the anxiety wasn't worth it.

## Custom Water Loop

So I "upgraded" to water cooling. Built a custom closed loop with a waterblock that had separate inlet and outlet ports designed to maximize pressure. It wasn't one of those maze-style blocks that runs water through channels — this one shoved water directly at the die. Which was fine for a Pentium 4, since P4s had a single die right in the center of the CPU. Direct impingement worked great for that layout.

My first waterblock came from taking apart a donor setup. Eventually I got proper fittings and tubing and built something that looked halfway professional. The P4 ran cool and quiet.

## AIO

At some point I grew up a little and switched to an all-in-one closed loop cooler. Less maintenance, no custom tubing, no worrying about leaks. It did the job. Not as fun, but practical.

## Back to Air

And then I grew up the rest of the way and went back to air cooling. A good tower cooler is quiet, has no moving parts besides one fan, never leaks, and performs within a few degrees of a 240mm AIO. Sometimes the boring answer is the right answer.

The watercooling parts eventually got sold off. I've got the photos somewhere of the whole collection laid out for sale — blocks, radiators, fittings, tubing. A whole era in a box.
