Technically this wasn't my first home server. That was an HTPC running Windows — the one I tried to get MythTV running on. It was my first real foray into Linux and I was quickly overwhelmed. Gave up and went back to Windows on that box. Now most of my devices run Linux.

But this Atom server was my first *real* Linux server. A little nettop with an Atom CPU, 4GB of RAM, a 2 or 3TB hard drive, and one 80GB SSD. It lived in a small square case tucked inside a cabinet with a couple of 120mm fans blowing air in and out. It moved from city to city with me. It had two whining sounds that drove me nuts. One was the CPU fan — expected. The other was the power supply, which was harder to identify. That high-pitched coil whine that you can only hear when the room is dead quiet. Which is exactly when you're trying to sleep with a server in the next room.

I had this massive heatsink I'd bought off eBay maybe 8 years earlier. One of those passive tower coolers that's absurdly oversized. I'd always wanted to use it for something. So I finally decided to see if I could make this thing completely fanless.

Getting the heatsink to mount was harder than I thought. The socket spacing didn't match, the mounting holes were wrong, and some components on the motherboard stuck up high enough to interfere with the heatsink base. I had to Dremel off some odd shapes from the bottom of the heatsink to clear them. Lots of very big hacks. Test fitting, grinding, test fitting again.

To my surprise, it worked. Thermals stayed within spec under normal load. Still buzzed a little from the PSU, but the fan noise was gone completely.

That Atom box eventually got replaced — I upgraded to a micro ATX board with an i3 and 8GB of RAM. That's my main home server now, the server I still use every day. Went from 8GB to 16GB this year. I just replaced that original 80GB SSD a couple months ago. The storage has grown from one small hard drive to two 22TB drives and a handful of 6TB drives — about 55TB total. Two power supplies later, it still does the job. Still fanless.
