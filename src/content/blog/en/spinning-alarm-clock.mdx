My ex loved this alarm clock but it was way too bright at night. Every single night she would make me get up and put a small sheet over it. Every. Night.

So I asked her what the solution could be. She turned down a small piece of cardboard that flaps up — too ugly. But she liked the idea of it automatically turning to face the wall at night.

I found one of those spinning platforms made for displaying jewelry, and that became the base. I reverse engineered the buttons and motor control, then shoved a NodeMCU inside it. Wired the whole thing up to Home Assistant so we could manually spin it any amount — 90 degrees, 180, whatever — from our phones.

![The jewelry display spinner — base before modification](/images/blog/spinning-alarm-clock/IMG_5786_spinner_base.jpg)

![NodeMCU wired into the spinner's control board](/images/blog/spinning-alarm-clock/IMG_5811_spinner_nodemcu.jpg)

Then I set up automations: at 8:45 PM it rotates to face the wall. At 6:00 AM it spins back. Problem solved. No more getting out of bed.

![Finished spinning alarm clock with the NodeMCU tucked inside](/images/blog/spinning-alarm-clock/IMG_5829_spinner_wiring.jpg)

The best part was reverse engineering the display's control board. The buttons were just pulling GPIO pins low, so I tapped into those traces and let the NodeMCU trigger them programmatically. The motor had enough torque to stop at precise angles, which I honestly didn't expect from a $15 jewelry spinner.
